# Manual visual review of the dataset

The review sheets (`review_genuine.png`, `review_counterfeit.png`) show a random sample of
40 images per class (seed 7, generated by `preprocessing/audit.py`). I counted by eye:

| Observation | Genuine (40) | Counterfeit (40) |
|---|---|---|
| Plain white/light background visible around the product (catalogue-style photo) | 26 | 3 |
| Chinese-language text visible on the packaging | 0 | 14 |
| Product fills almost the whole frame (close-up crop) | ~12 | ~37 |

Other points from the review:

* **Genuine images** are mostly online-pharmacy catalogue photos of Indian products
  (Shelcal, Ecosprin, Calpol, paracetamol brands). Several carry website watermarks such as
  "PharmEasy", "medicaldawa.in" and "TNMEDS". A handful of brands make up much of the class.
* **Counterfeit images** are screenshots (file names `Screenshot 2025-09-17 ...png`),
  mostly tight crops of blister packs and boxes. They include Chinese products and
  Philippine brands (e.g. Alaxan FR, Neozep, Medicol, Decolgen).
* **Neither sample contains a brand that appears in the other class.** The classes
  therefore differ in *which products* they show, not only in genuine vs counterfeit.
* File-level facts (from `audit_summary_by_class.csv`): all genuine files are JPEG,
  median 225x225 px, about 6 KB, and lie on a constant-pixel-count line (about 50,000 px),
  which fits web image-search thumbnails. All counterfeit files are PNG, median 452x550 px,
  about 324 KB.

**Consequence:** a model can separate the classes by source, framing, background,
language or brand, without learning anything about counterfeiting. The accuracy numbers in
this project measure how separable **this dataset** is. They are not a measure of real-world
counterfeit detection.
